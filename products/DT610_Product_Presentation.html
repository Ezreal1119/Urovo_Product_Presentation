Sources & configuration notes

DT610 / SQ610 / Standard Edition. Evidence reviewed 13 October 2026. These notes retain qualification detail while keeping the two customer pages readable.

[1] Integrated RFID and nearby verification

Dedicated DT610 specification: Impinj Gen2X, up to 1.5 m and 50+ tags/s. The series sheet and user guide use 50 tags/s. Treat these as stated capability limits, not guaranteed field results. Tag size, orientation, metal/liquid, nearby inventory and application filtering affect the result. Gen2X gains require compatible tags as well as a compatible reader.

The user guide says internal RFID is disabled by default and enabled through the SDK. Prepare the actual application before demonstration. Returns, item lookup and replenishment are proposed workflows requiring back-end item records and exception rules.

Standard-range 1D/2D capture is standard. Dedicated specification lists optional FlexRange from 5 cm to 10 m. No claim of universal 10 m capture or specialist DPM performance is made.

[3] Front-display NFC and SoftPOS

The dedicated specification lists on-screen NFC and EMVCo PCD L1. The product announcement links it to SoftPayment and requires an app. September 10 memory identifies DT610 as front-only NFC with no side switch, explicitly explaining its SoftPOS positioning. Some older guides/series material describes different NFC faces, so confirm the shipping build.

Front tapping keeps the amount and customer instruction visible. L1 interface evidence does not itself establish a fully approved payment solution. Confirm provider support for the exact device/OS build, merchant eligibility, integrity checks, receipts, refunds and supported cards/wallets. Standard DT610 Apple VAS loyalty support is not established by the reviewed sources.

[4] Wireless platform

Local specification lists 5G NSA/SA Sub-6, Wi-Fi 7 2×2 MU-MIMO with DBS, and Bluetooth 6.0. World and North America cellular bands differ. AP, carrier, regional spectrum, firmware and back-end response determine actual application performance. The supplied document’s fixed latency/capacity multipliers are not presented as customer guarantees.

[5] FP licensing, memory and AI software

Memory states default FP1 at 2.0 GHz and optional licensed FP4 at 2.9 GHz, on the same Q-6690 hardware. The dedicated spec lists 6 INT8 TOPS, 8 GB / 128 GB UFS and optional 12 GB / 256 GB. Neither maximum clock nor TOPS is an application-speed guarantee.

The revised page uses “Qualcomm QCM-6690, up to 2.9 GHz” as user-directed portfolio positioning, not as a claim of universal market leadership or a measured advantage across every licensed configuration. The spec headline benchmark (1,114,080) conflicts with internal FP1/FP4 values. It is deliberately excluded. OCR and AI tools require their actual release, license and firmware support; some portfolio utilities are marked coming soon.

[6] Battery choices, real runtime evidence and hot-swap

Dedicated sources state 4000 mAh standard and 4950 mAh optional. The old battlecard’s 6000 mAh is not treated as a confirmed current option. The user guide requires main-battery replacement within 60 seconds. Heavy apps, temperature and consecutive swaps can shorten bridge availability. Calls terminate during replacement.

Dated lab reference: 4000 mAh recorded 10 h 20 min; 4950 mAh recorded 14 h 30 min. Conditions include a 3-second scan interval, 320-nit brightness, Wi-Fi and Bluetooth enabled, 25°C. Scanner-engine, cellular and audio cells are blank for DT610. These are not guaranteed shift lengths or continuous-RFID/payment results.

[8] Protection, display and identity

[9] Optional RFG91 and dimensioning opportunities

The dedicated RFG91 specification lists the E710 reader, 15 m+ nominal range and 1,300+ tags/s stated maximum. These are sled figures, not DT610’s internal-reader figures. The DT610 pogo mount requires its rugged case. Confirm regional RFID, tags, app reader selection, combined ergonomics and the actual sled variant.

Product memory also describes an external rear structured-light camera clip for DT610 parcel dimensioning. No dedicated synchronized module specification or measurement accuracy was established, so this remains a qualified presales opportunity rather than a main-page commitment. [10] AI Vision and optional 3D dimensioning

The DT610 standard specification lists 6 INT8 TOPS and AI Vision Multi-Barcode. “Batch-identify barcoded products” means identifying multiple visible codes in one frame; it does not claim general object recognition without barcodes. Application support and field accuracy should be demonstrated on the actual build.

Product memory records a rear POGO-connected structured-light clip for both DT610 and DT610 Pro. The dedicated Pro specification confirms optional 3D sensing for volume measurement; the standard DT610 specification does not list the module. Treat it as an optional solution for either model, subject to confirmation of the orderable clip, firmware, application, calibration and measurement performance for the actual deployment.

[11] Competitor evidence and selection boundaries

The supplied comparison image and Markdown informed the competitor set. Current official vendor specifications were checked before using customer-facing figures. DT610's 2.9 GHz ceiling is the optional FP4 licensed performance mode; its published 222 g weight is for the standard battery. Published weights are not necessarily the carried solution weight.

  • Zebra EM45 RFID specification and feature guide: 250 g, Wi-Fi 6E, service-replaceable battery and camera barcode capture.
  • Bluebird X40 official page: camera capture, Wi-Fi 6E, warm-swap, 199 g, AMOLED and 50 MP rear camera. This is why DT610 is not described as the lightest.
  • Zebra TC201 official specification: Q-6690 up to 2.0 GHz, 245 g with the standard battery, Wi-Fi 7, 5G and integrated short-range UHF on select SKUs. The official sheet lists contactless-payment NFC, so DT610's front-display placement is a workflow difference rather than a claim that TC201 lacks payment NFC. The supplied image's TC201 rear-camera figure differs from the current official sheet and is not repeated.

No universal price, benchmark, repair-rate or TCO superiority is claimed. Compare the actual workflow, complete configuration, software and commercial support.
