[2] Integrated scanner versus camera-based capture

The September spec lists a standard-range 1D/2D scan engine with FlexRange as an option. Local mapping and accessory workbook identify standard SE2030 S1-SR620W and optional SE2030 FR, but the slide stays at the configuration-neutral description in the new spec.

A dedicated barcode engine uses scanning optics and an aiming/illumination system rather than relying only on the general imaging camera. The practical advantage is a separate integrated capture mechanism for item workflows. Successful decode still depends on label size, condition, distance and configuration; no measured speed or success-rate superiority over Samsung camera software is claimed.

Demonstrate: use the customer’s actual product and screen codes, confirm the ID in the stock/POS record and test repetitive scans. If FlexRange is needed, verify the precise supported engine and label/distance combination. No UPad-specific maximum optical scan distance is inferred from another UROVO product.

[3] Optional integrated UHF for nearby stock and assets

September spec, page 5: optional built-in UHF RFID, up to 1.5 m read range and 50 tags/s stated read rate. The July accessory-system spec adds EPC C1 Gen2 / ISO 18000-6C and regional 902–928 / 865–868 MHz ranges but omits the optional qualifier; the internal mapping also says built-in RFID without a configuration qualifier. The 1.5 m maximum and nominal tag-rate figures do not guarantee the same performance on all tags or at maximum range simultaneously. Tag design, placement, material, RF power, region and environment matter. Short range alone does not guarantee isolation of only the intended item. Identify unique tags and reconcile with business records in the application.

Demonstrate: read a representative nearby product group, compare unique IDs against the stock record and identify missed/unexpected items. Adjust power and filtering. Do not position this configuration as a substitute for a verified long-range warehouse pistol reader.

[6] Current battery wording and engineering measurements

September spec: user non-replaceable 3.87 V 8000 mAh / 30.96 Wh battery; quick charging; 18 W 9 V/2 A wall adapter. This supersedes the older May spec’s “service replaceable” field for the visible deck. Do not offer hot-swap, tool-free battery exchange or user servicing. “User non-replaceable” does not establish that authorized service can never replace a battery.

The user’s engineering memory mem_d8914edf6900b797 records 1%–100% charging at about 4 h 09 min on 5 V/2 A and 2 h 31 min on 9 V/2 A; a roughly 9.68-hour run with Wi-Fi, 320-nit screen on, maximum volume and one scan every three seconds. These are dated engineering conditions, not a runtime guarantee for the September revision. The main slide uses no operating-hours promise.

The same memory distinguishes ports: bottom USB-C 3.1 supports PC data transfer; top/right USB-C and pogo contacts support OTG use but not PC transfer. Do not treat “all interfaces support OTG” as identical PC/data/video functionality.

Demonstrate: check charging in the actual station and measure the intended workload. Keep tablet charging, station power and the older printer-dock adapter separate; the July accessory document’s 24 V/3 A output was marked to be determined.
