SP35 · Sources & presentation notes

Evidence checked 13 October 2026. Product facts lead from the September 2 dedicated spec; original illustrations come from the supplied UROVO announcement. These are application illustrations, not customer-case evidence.

[1] Professional barcode capture and cart auto-scan

The dedicated spec lists standard-range 1D/2D scanning and an optional FlexRange engine. The September accessory guide identifies UROVO SE2030S as the standard engine. The supplied announcement, slides 7–8, shows damaged-label capture and the terminal mounted vertically in a white shopping-cart holder for auto-scan. The user guide describes hands-free continuous scan configuration. The visible slides do not promise every optional engine, a scan distance, Digimarc or a decode-speed advantage.

The hardware captures item identifiers. Basket updates, duplicate-scan handling, quantity changes, promotions and purchase reconciliation depend on the retail application and item database. The cart holder is shown in official artwork; an artwork image is not proof of an included, available mount SKU.

Demonstrate: use the proposed cart mount and the customer’s grocery labels, including reflective packaging and representative damaged codes. Add an item, intentionally present it twice, change a quantity and remove it in the shopping app. Confirm the product/cart record rather than demonstrating a successful beep alone.

[2] Front NFC, membership and SoftPOS

The dedicated spec explicitly lists Front NFC for membership, checkout and SoftPOS. The announcement, slide 9, shows a smartphone and contactless bank card tapping near the top/front of the display. The older battlecard calls this under-screen NFC. This deck uses front-facing NFC; it does not claim a rear or top-edge antenna or multiple sensing surfaces.

SoftPOS is a supported solution capability, not an assertion that an NFC reader by itself is a complete, certified payment terminal. The payment provider must support the exact SP35 firmware, security/certification requirements, region, acquirer and transaction flow. Integration of basket totals, payment confirmation and receipt handling belongs to the retail/POS solution. No universal bank-card acceptance, PCI PTS/EMV certificate, bundled payment app or payment processing service is asserted.

The current local material lists compatible MIFARE and NFC tag families. Card reading is distinct from secure membership authentication and account binding. Validate the customer’s actual cards and application.

Demonstrate: first identify a member; then complete a permitted demonstration transaction with the approved provider. Show the transaction status and cart reconciliation. The slide’s official bank-card artwork illustrates the intended front-tap interaction; it does not establish live acceptance.

[3] Optional UWB navigation and device tracking

The current spec and announcement identify UWB as optional. The spec describes product locating, route guidance and real-time device tracking for up to 48 hours after power-off. The announcement also describes centimeter-level positioning. The latter is not treated as a guaranteed accuracy in every aisle, so the visible slide uses indoor positioning without a numeric accuracy promise.

Product finding needs a deployed positioning solution, a mapped store, item-location data and an integrated application. Tracking after power-off is a stated capability with a limited duration; it does not imply tracking with a removed/depleted battery or without an operational positioning infrastructure. Site coverage, obstruction, calibration and solution configuration affect results. Customer-location sharing for staff assistance needs the associated workflow and location integration.

Demonstrate: select an item in the shopping list, follow the mapped route, request assistance and verify that the correct shopper location reaches the staff workflow. Separately validate misplaced-device recovery and the proposed power-off tracking configuration.

[4] One-press staff assistance and audio

The dedicated spec names a side function button configurable as PTT, with one-press assistance and customer-location support. It lists two noise-canceling microphones, one speaker and Bluetooth-headset support. The action key defaults to PTT. UTalk 2.0 appears in the UEE software list. The announcement’s slide 3 explicitly illustrates one-press staff assistance.

The key is an input to the configured application. It does not automatically create a service-desk queue or transmit a shopper location without supporting software and location services. Noise cancellation supports voice capture; no measured reduction in wait time or noise level is claimed.

Demonstrate: make a shopper request from a noisy aisle, route it to the right staff group and confirm who accepts the request. Verify voice intelligibility and location behavior on the proposed build.

[5] Current platform, memory and OS path

Current dedicated spec: Qualcomm Q-6690, octa-core 2.9 GHz; 6 GB RAM / 64 GB storage; Android 15, upgradable to Android 20. The supplied announcement slide 4 identifies the 4 nm Qualcomm Dragonwing Q-6690; slide 12 agrees with 6+64. Android 20 is the documented upgrade target, not an assertion that Android 20 is available now, included under every contract or maintained on a particular schedule.

Active discrepancies: announcement slide 2 still says 8 GB RAM; the older battlecard and internal mapping retain 8+128/4+64 configurations. The September 2 guide change record and spec specify 6+64. The accessory guide’s 790K AnTuTu sample still names 8+128, so it is not applied to the current 6+64 configuration or used as a sales performance benchmark.

The user-designated memory was searched for SP35 and relevant shared technology; it contains no SP35-specific entry. Memory mem_f4d805388e0b69c3 describes DT610 Q-6690 FP1/FP4 license-controlled performance tiers. That DT610-specific factory default must not be inherited by SP35 without evidence. This deck names the SP35 platform and does not promise an application speedup or an FP license entitlement.

Demonstrate: run the proposed shopping and supported payment applications together, with the store’s real dataset. Obtain the quoted configuration and OS maintenance terms before committing to a lifecycle or upgrade schedule.

[6] Wi-Fi-only connectivity and regional 6 GHz conditions

Dedicated spec: Wi-Fi 6E with 2×2 MU-MIMO across 2.4/5/6 GHz, Bluetooth 5.3 with configurable Class 1/Class 2, Classic BR/EDR and BLE. The announcement lists Class 2. Only the shared non-conflicting Bluetooth version appears on the main slide.

The canonical shared platform mapping identifies SP35 as Q-6690 Wi-Fi-only. Generic Android SIM/eSIM/mobile-data screenshots in the user guide do not establish cellular hardware. This is an in-store WLAN device; the deck does not offer 4G, 5G or eSIM.

6 GHz use needs compatible access points, suitable deployment and local regulatory permission. It can add WLAN capacity, but the hardware generation alone does not prove better latency, roaming, throughput or simultaneous-user counts. The slide does not copy “zero latency” or “uninterrupted” from marketing copy.

Demonstrate: keep an active shopping session while moving between the customer’s access points; test the checkout and assistance flows near coverage boundaries and the device issue/return bank.

[7] 4900 mAh standard pack and stated runtime

Current spec: user-replaceable 3.6 V 4900 mAh standard battery, 3500 mAh optional. It states 12-hour runtime and AI-driven battery management. The announcement says “over 12 hours” and describes smart charging diagnostics detecting faulty devices. The slide conservatively uses the spec’s stated 12 hours, with workload qualification; it does not promise a full trading day under every workload.

Battery replacement in the user guide uses a PH00 screwdriver, handle cover and cable connector. This is not described as a tool-free exchange, hot-swap or seamless shopper-session change. Schedule battery servicing and recharging around the managed device pool. Replaceability also helps replace an aging pack without replacing the entire terminal.

Charging time in the appended cradle manual is about four hours for 0–100%. Do not confuse 12 V / 10 A / 120 W cradle-adapter specifications with handheld charging power. The 40% nominal capacity difference versus a 3500 mAh PS30 pack is arithmetic, not proof of 40% longer runtime.

Demonstrate: monitor charge and battery health over a representative shopping workload, including display, scans, WLAN traffic, payment and optional positioning. Size the pool and charging plan from measured usage.

[8] Retail handling protection and limits

Dedicated spec: IP65; 1.5 m drop to tile over concrete across -10°C to +50°C, MIL-STD-810H; 1000 × 0.5 m tumbles at room temperature. These are documented test conditions, not damage-free use, a warranty for every fall or an immersion rating.

IP65 supports dust protection and water-jet resistance. Avoid translating it into submersion, chemical/disinfectant compatibility, cold-chain suitability or IP67/IP68. Follow the user guide for cleaning and dry charging contacts before docking.

Customer value: a dedicated terminal with defined handling tests is suitable for repeated non-specialist customer use. Do not claim a percentage reduction in breakages or maintenance without customer evidence.

[9] Shopper display and camera

Dedicated spec: 5.0-inch 1280×720, optional 1920×1080, 400 nits; multimode capacitive bare-finger touch, water-droplet rejection and anti-smudge coating. Rear 16 MP PDAF camera with flash and torch mode. No front camera or universal glove support is asserted.

The screen is a workspace for item details, shopping lists, basket totals and directions supplied by the application. The rear camera can create visual records for product checks and returns; automated visual recognition needs supported software, data and validation. Camera resolution does not by itself prove recognition accuracy or successful recovery of every unreadable barcode.

Current spec weight is 302/278 g; the older battlecard says 310 g. Because the two current weights are not explicitly mapped to configurations, no weight figure is used as a competitive advantage in the visible slides.

[10] Membership release, locking and maintainable charging

The supplied announcement slide 10 shows membership-card unlock on the docked SP35 or a nearby kiosk and an auto-lock cradle. The dedicated spec describes the open-base design reducing debris and modular pogo pins for field maintenance. The user guide’s appended manual identifies three terminal slots and permits daisy-chaining three cradles from one specified official adapter.

The member/session/device association is a retail-application workflow. A mechanical lock supports device-pool control; it does not guarantee prevention of theft, loss or misuse. The optional UWB tracking solution is separate from the lock.

Availability discrepancy: the September 18 accessory workbook still marks the three-slot cradle, keys and service tools as “in development”, with most codes/material numbers absent, while the spec and user guide list the cradle and the user guide includes it in unpacking. Confirm production availability, exact kit contents, mount and cradle SKUs and delivery dates for the project. The visible slide therefore does not call the cradle an included, immediately available standard kit.

The manual contains both generic “charging state” wording and a specific LED table identifying lock/unlock/error states. Use the specific table: cradle indicator off without a terminal, red unlock error, green lock/unlock indication; battery charging state is checked at the terminal. This deck does not promise that every cradle light is a charge indicator.

Demonstrate: release an authorized device, cancel an unauthorized request, insert the terminal to lock and charge, show the terminal charge status and explain the local pogo-module service procedure.

[11] Selected competitor differences and source corrections

The synchronized SP35 Battlecard names Zebra PS30 and PS20. It is a dated comparison aid, not a current competitor specification. This deck limits visible comparisons to specific differences corroborated against official Zebra specifications on 13 October 2026.

Zebra PS30 official specifications: 4.7-inch 1280×720 display and a 3500 mAh battery. SP35’s standard 4900 mAh pack is 40% higher in nominal capacity. This is not a measured runtime comparison. Screen diagonal does not establish better brightness or optics; PS30 lists 540 nits versus SP35’s 400. PS30 also supports hands-free scanning, front NFC in Plus configurations, location services and staff assistance, so these are not claimed as uniquely absent from PS30.

Zebra PS20 official specifications: 4.0-inch WVGA display and 802.11ac WLAN. The SP35 visible row compares its 5-inch HD display and Wi-Fi 6E platform, while retaining network/regional conditions.

Correction to supplied synthesis/battlecard: PS20 Plus does have a 5 MP front-facing camera, and PS20 supports PTT/locationing. Neither is described as absent across the PS20 family. The deck avoids stale competitor OS ceilings, questionable dimensional/weight entries, an assertion that only SP35 can navigate/pay/request help, and claims of universal platform superiority.

The official PS30 page lists Digimarc and reuse of existing Zebra shopper cradles. SP35’s loaded local spec does not confirm Digimarc or compatibility with Zebra cradles. Validate these migration requirements before offering replacement. No universal cost, runtime, decode-speed or ROI advantage is asserted.

[12] Workforce workflows and fleet tools

The dedicated spec explicitly lists click-and-collect picking, store inventory, stock/price checks, replenishment, returns, assisted sales and mPOS queue busting. These are supported application directions, not bundled business applications or named customer deployments. The shopper-focused device is positioned for in-store work; it is not a substitute for an unverified warehouse/cold-chain/UHF requirement.
