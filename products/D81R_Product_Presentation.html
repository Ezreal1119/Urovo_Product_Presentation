Sources & configuration notes

D81R Series · D812R / D813R · source checks: November 2, 2026

Controlling sources and model selection

The dedicated D81R Series(EN)-SPEC-20250114 specification (PDF modified September 24, 2026) and D81R Series(EN)-PB-2025 brochure control the hard specifications. D812R: 203 dpi, 104 mm maximum print width. D813R: 300 dpi, 108.4 mm maximum print width. Both share thermal transfer/direct thermal printing, 1,000 mm maximum print length, 500 MHz 32-bit processor, 8 MB RAM and 16 MB Flash, sensors and the listed interface options. The RAM subtype differs between the spec (PSRAM) and brochure (SDRAM), so no RAM subtype is advertised.

The newer A3 user manual, revision dated September 24, 2026, correctly names D812R / D813R and lists print-only maxima of 200 / 150 mm/s. These are separate from RFID print-and-encode maxima, which are 4 in/s for both. Resolution supports choosing layouts with finer detail; no throughput benefit is asserted for D812R when both models are printing and encoding RFID labels.

Print and encode performance and pitch

The dedicated spec and brochure explicitly list maximum RFID speed 4 inch/s with printing and writing at the same time. This is a maximum, not a guaranteed rate for every tag, data length, verification, locking, retry or finishing mode. Print-only mode and read/write-before-printing modes are also documented. The in-one-pass feature does not establish that all jobs have zero backfeed under all conditions.

The specification gives minimum on-pitch label length as 15 mm with an inconsistent parenthesis of 0.625 inches. 0.625 inches equals 15.875 mm, not 15 mm. The brochure and A3 manual also list 15 mm, so the slide retains the stated metric value and labels it as specified, but does not claim a verified pitch advantage over 15.9 mm or 16 mm competitor figures. Validate the delivered antenna/media setup on the target inlay.

Protocol: ISO18000-6C / EPC Class 1 Gen2. The chip list is compatibility guidance, not a guarantee for every inlay geometry or region. No unlisted RF frequency or global certification coverage is inferred. The brochure's Impinj Connected badge is not treated as proof of a particular chipset or universal RFID compatibility.

On-metal media and source differences

The dedicated spec lists 20–118 mm media width and 0.06–1.3 mm media thickness, with optional compatibility for flexible anti-metal tags no thicker than 1.3 mm. The brochure supports RFID tags, flexible anti-metal labels, jewelry adhesive labels and coated paper labels. Ordinary paper can be printed without implying that it contains a UHF chip.

The A3 manual and shared printer table include a conflicting 1.5 mm anti-metal statement, while the A3 paper-thickness row still says up to 1.3 mm. The slide conservatively follows the dedicated spec at up to 1.3 mm with the matching option and validated media. The brochure says less than 1.3 mm on its cover. Exact borderline stock must be tested. No claim is made that all rigid tags fit, that all configurations include the anti-metal option, or that other RFID printers cannot handle anti-metal labels.

Other source differences: D813R is 108.4 mm in the dedicated spec/brochure and 108 mm in the A3/shared table; maximum media width is 118 mm versus 120 mm; roll core is 25.4 mm versus a wider 25–76 mm accessory-table range. The deck uses dedicated-source values and avoids promising additional core adaptors.

Calibration and profile reuse

One-click or tool calibration obtains RFID read/write power and position. Saved RFID profiles can be reused for previously calibrated labels on the same printer. This does not establish that one profile transfers to all printers, all batches or different inlays without testing. Apply the documented calibration procedure before validating throughput.

Read-back and failed-tag handling

The spec lists TID, EPC or TID+EPC reading, RFID password lock/unlock, failed-label marking and configurable rewrite/reprint handling. These are RFID functions; they do not establish optical verification of the printed barcode, automatic compatibility with every chip's lock state, tamper-proof records or a completed end-to-end audit system. No guarantee of zero failed labels is made.

Languages, software and integration

The dedicated specification lists TSPL, EPL, ZPL and ZPL II. The brochure and shared printer configuration table list TSPL, ZPL and ZPL II; those shared three are shown in the presentation. The A3 manual/shared configuration table lists driver printing and Windows, iOS and Android secondary-development SDKs. The standard box includes a Windows labeling-software CD. Command emulation does not guarantee every existing template or RFID command works unchanged; test the customer format, tag data and selected app/driver combination.

Interfaces, accessories and finishing

Standard interface: USB. Dedicated-spec options: Ethernet 10/100 Mbps, Bluetooth/Wi-Fi, and combined network configurations. The older spec lists 2.4 GHz Wi-Fi and Bluetooth 2.1 + 4.2; the newer A3 manual lists USB 2.0, Bluetooth SPP+BLE 4.2 and 2.4/5 GHz Wi-Fi options. The visible page therefore says Wi-Fi / Bluetooth without claiming an exact radio option for every unit.

Standard pack: Windows labeling-software CD, quick start guide, USB cable, power cord, two 1-inch ribbon spindles for 300 m ribbon, and external universal switching power supply (24 V / 2.5 A / 60 W). Maximum ribbon length is 300 m, outside diameter 67 mm, 1-inch core; label roll outside diameter is 127 mm. Ribbon spindles do not mean that a 300 m ribbon roll is included.

The shared Device and Accessories Guide, printer configuration sheet, explicitly lists optional full cutter and peeler for D812R and D813R. Their existence does not guarantee compatibility with maximum-thickness on-metal media or every RFID inlay; check the chosen stock, finishing method and order code. Display is not included. No undocumented ribbon saving, automatic cutter, industrial duty cycle, spare-parts interchangeability or price advantage is claimed.

Desktop competitors and matched configurations

Zebra ZD621R official support and the manufacturer-authored ZD621R/ZD611R datasheet document 203 / 300 dpi configurations, a 4.3-inch colour touch display, USB and Ethernet, and both 300 m and 74 m ribbon support. D81R's 300 m ribbon is therefore a shared feature, not an exclusive advantage. Zebra's print-only maximum speed is not compared directly with D81R's 4 in/s RFID print-and-write figure. Different published Zebra datasheet revisions give different 300 dpi print widths; no Zebra print-width superiority claim is made.

Zebra official ZD421/ZD621 user guide also lists 300 m and 74 m ribbon options. Manufacturer datasheet reference copy: Zebra ZD621 RFID datasheet.

TSC official TH240/DH240 Series user manual, PDF pages 11 and 14 (printed pages 8 and 11): TH240T 203 dpi / 108 mm, TH340T 300 dpi / 105.7 mm. The thermal-transfer family supports 300 m ribbon. Its UHF RFID configuration supports selected on-metal tags up to 1.35 mm, tear-off mode only, with minimum label/tag pitch 15.9 mm. UHF is a selected RFID configuration, not standard on every TH240T/TH340T, and is not applicable to the RC ribbon-cartridge variant. The visible table labels these as RFID configurations and preserves the selected-tag and tear-off conditions.

TSC is a direct desktop alternative with its own strengths, not an ordinary printer that cannot feed on-metal tags. SATO CL4NX Plus is an industrial-class reference and is omitted from this compact desktop comparison. No matched price, service cost, ROI, universal performance advantage or promise of a direct drop-in replacement is asserted.
