Sources & configuration notes

RT40S / SQ47S. Local product sources, user-supplied analysis and accumulated product memory; competitor vendor specifications reviewed 13 October 2026.

[1] Cold-chain configuration and heating

The dedicated specification lists −30°C to +50°C for the cold-chain version with screen and imager-window heaters; the regular version is −20°C to +50°C. Internal mapping limits cold-chain options to 38- and 51-key models; 29 keys is ambient only. The shared accessories guide lists a low-temperature 5200 mAh battery.

Choose the exact cold-chain device, battery and key layout for the site. Heating can help keep the viewing and scan surfaces usable, but does not guarantee that every temperature transition is condensation-free. Evaluate the full ambient/freezer cycle. The generic Heating App document is not model-specific, so its automation modes are not promised as included RT40S features.

[2] Scan engine and distance

The RT40S brochure states up to 15 m; this is used as the conservative marketing ceiling for the selected extended-range option. Internal mapping lists SE2030/SE4770 standard-range, SE5500 and SE5800 choices. Product memory records RT40S as the UROVO PDA supporting the long-range SE5800 class, with up to 30 m on a large 100 mil UPC-A and about 4 m on a 13 mil UPC-A. These values describe different engine/label conditions and must not be merged into a universal read-distance guarantee.

Confirm the orderable engine and test actual rack, pallet and carton labels. Reduced walking, bending or repositioning is a workflow objective, not a quantified customer result.

[3] Keypad and carrying format

The specification lists a 4-inch 480×800 screen, 38-key keypad with 29/51-key choices, and a 199×58×29 mm body. The product image shows the supplied 38-key version. Physical-key efficiency depends on app focus, shortcuts and operator familiarity; map common WMS fields to the selected layout. The 51-key count is not itself evidence that every application is faster.

[4] Hot-swap and shift planning

The dedicated spec lists a removable 3.85 V, 5200 mAh battery with hot-swap. Portfolio brochure wording describes replacing without reboot. No 60-second bridge duration from another model is copied here. Follow the RT40S procedure and verify task state on the deployed firmware.

The accessories guide documents device/battery charging cradles; the newer shared guide adds a 20-slot battery charger. These are optional. A dated RT40S lab record lists 12 h 22 min under its ambient scan-test conditions and 3 h 57 min charging at 5 V/2 A; those are not cold-room runtime guarantees. The spec’s sub-three-hour/QC3.0 claim depends on the charging setup, and region-specific adapter sources differ.

[5] Environmental protection

The dedicated specification lists IP68 and 1.8 m drops onto flat concrete. These laboratory ratings do not guarantee survival of every accident, chemical cleaning or every thermal transition. The spec says 1000 tumbles at 1 m; accumulated memory separately records a 500-cycle observed result. The slide omits that disputed tumble figure.

[6] WLAN, cellular and platform

RT40S supports Wi-Fi 6 on 2.4/5 GHz, 2×2 MU-MIMO, 802.11r, Bluetooth 5.0 and regional 2G/3G/4G. It does not support 5G or Wi-Fi 6E. Fast roaming requires compatible WLAN and security configuration; application behavior and coverage still determine task continuity.

The baseline is Android 13, Qualcomm octa-core 2.45 GHz and 4/64 GB. Chip naming differs across the local sources, so no specific chipset alias is used. Memory says SQ47Pro is discontinued and SQ47S is planned for Android 16 firmware in 2027; the old comparison says Android 15. Future OS dates and service commitments are therefore not promised on these pages.

[8] NFC read/write

The spec explicitly lists 13.56 MHz HF read/write with ISO15693, ISO14443 A/B, MIFARE and FeliCa. A compatible tag and app are needed for tool/container identification and updates. This is not long-range UHF inventory, precise asset location or proof of an approved payment solution. PSAM/SIM slot availability must be confirmed separately.

[9] Competitive configuration boundaries

The supplied analysis and local dated battlecards informed model selection. Current primary vendor sources qualify the final comparison; N7 Cachalot Pro II is explicitly used rather than transferring the old N7/Android 11 row.

  • Zebra MC3400/MC3450 specification: IP65/IP67, 29/38/47-key choices, Wi-Fi 6E and 7000 mAh pack; MC3450 has 5G. Hot-swap is also supported, so it is not a unique RT40S advantage.
  • Honeywell CK62 official page and datasheet: IP65, −20°C minimum, Wi-Fi 6E and selected 5G options. RT40S cold-chain is optional; do not compare an ambient RT40S as if it has heaters.
  • Newland N7 Cachalot Pro II official page: IP65, 29/38/47 keys, Android 13, hot-swap and Duo Near & Far scanning. No exclusive RT40S hot-swap or Android-13 advantage is claimed.

No universal price, TCO, battery-runtime, CPU-performance or overall ruggedness superiority is asserted. Select RT40S for its keypad, heated cold-chain configuration and appropriate scan engine; projects requiring 5G, 6 GHz WLAN or a higher-memory platform need a different product evaluation.
