Sources & configuration notes

DT630 / SQ630. Evidence reviewed 13 October 2026. The two slides are customer-facing; this panel preserves source qualifications, practical demonstration guidance and unresolved conflicts.

[1] Dedicated rear scanning: the principal capture difference

The dedicated specification identifies a rear-positioned professional standard-range 1D/2D scan engine and line-of-sight operation. Internal mapping identifies UROVO SE630. Product memory states that SE630 is rear-mounted and uses green LED without a laser. This is separate from the 50 MP photography camera. LED/no-laser wording is not a formal eye-safety certification or a claim of universal safety superiority.

Demonstrate: use actual customer labels and a side trigger to complete a repeated scan-and-confirm task, including normal lighting and awkward label positions. Show the interaction, not an unsupported percentage speed improvement. No FlexRange, 10 m reading, DPM qualification or pallet-distance claim is established for DT630.

[2] Photography and evidence capture

The dedicated spec lists a 50 MP autofocus rear camera with PDAF, flash/torch, an 8 MP fixed-focus front camera, and highlights 4K video and low-light image enhancement. No Optical Image Stabilization (OIS) is claimed for DT630. OCR of printed labels or forms is a possible application of captured images when compatible recognition software is deployed; it is not a built-in camera function or a verified accuracy guarantee.

Why it matters: the barcode links a record to an item; a photo documents the exception or condition. The delivery/inspection workflow is a presales application proposal, requiring the business app to associate image, item, operator and time. There is no verified named DT630 customer case or measured dispute-reduction ROI in the reviewed sources.

Demonstrate: scan a parcel, photograph its damage, and retrieve the linked record. Check capture detail, upload time, retention/privacy rules and offline synchronization in the actual app.

[3] Compute, NPU and software readiness

Dedicated specification: 4 nm octa-core 2.5 GHz platform, dedicated NPU, 8 GB RAM / 128 GB storage. The AnTuTu score is 950,707 using V11; it is excluded from the main slides because cross-version and cross-vendor benchmark comparisons do not establish workflow performance.

An NPU accelerates compatible neural-network inference, such as image or voice models. Hardware readiness does not mean that every AI feature is installed, licensed or fully offline. The UEE portfolio lists OCR, AI Vision/multibarcode and AI Voice utilities; availability, firmware, language/model support, processing location and licensing must be checked individually. Do not quote DT610 feature packs, 12/256 GB or a borrowed TOPS figure for DT630.

Demonstrate: keep the customer's inventory app, form and communications app active, then test switching and capture. Demonstrate AI only on a supported shipping build and the actual proposed license.

[4] Optional built-in UHF and local stock workflows

The specification explicitly says optional built-in UHF, with Impinj Gen2X in the highlights and 50 tags/s in the data-capture table. Page 2 says over 50 tags/s and 1.2 m; page 5 says 50 tags/s and up to 1.5 m. The read-distance conflict is unresolved. The slides deliberately omit a distance promise and use 50 tags/s as a stated capability, not guaranteed throughput.

UHF can identify multiple tagged items without individually aligning each barcode. A nearby verification workflow needs the appropriate tags, reader variant, regional RF configuration, item database, filtering and exception handling. Tag orientation, material, metal/liquid and nearby inventory affect performance. Gen2X benefits require compatible tags and solution implementation.

Demonstrate: read a small known batch, verify expected versus unexpected items, then show barcode fallback. Do not present nearby reading as full warehouse inventory, precise item location, or a long-range sled capability. DT630/RFG91 mechanical compatibility is not established by the reviewed DT630 accessories guide.

[5] Sealing and drop conditions

The dedicated specification lists IP68; 1.5 m bare-device / 1.8 m with rugged case drops to tile over concrete at 25°C, per MIL-STD-810H. For −10°C to +60°C, it lists 1.2 m bare / 1.5 m with case. It also lists 1,000 × 0.5 m tumbles at room temperature without the boot, operating −20°C to +60°C and non-condensing humidity.

The drop heights shown on page 1 are qualified at 25°C. This is not a universal drop guarantee or evidence of oil/chemical compatibility, disinfectant suitability, freezer condensation tolerance or hazardous-area/ATEX approval. Demonstrations should use approved samples and procedures rather than improvised drop/water testing.

[6] Wireless, positioning and field tasks

Dedicated spec: 5G Sub-6 NSA/SA, Wi-Fi 6E 2×2 MU-MIMO, Bluetooth 5.4 and concurrent L1/L5 multi-constellation GNSS. Dual-frequency GNSS offers more signal information for compatible positioning; it does not establish a guaranteed location accuracy. Region-specific cellular bands, private LTE/CBRS, eSIM provisioning, AP support and legal 6 GHz availability need deployment checks.

Why it matters: business apps can exchange task and evidence data over site Wi-Fi or cellular. Automatic lossless network handoff, zero latency, offline operation and guaranteed upload speed are not implied. The app must implement any offline queue/synchronization required by the workflow.

Demonstrate: complete a task over the customer network, upload the expected photo size and test a coverage transition. Check regional carrier compatibility and Bluetooth accessories.

[7] Replaceable power: power-off procedure, not hot-swap

Dedicated specification and accessories guide agree on a 4500 mAh / 17.46 Wh / 3.88 V user-replaceable battery and an 18 W (9 V / 2 A) adapter. Smart battery management is described. The user guide explicitly instructs powering off before replacing the battery. The slides do not claim hot-swap, warm-swap or uninterrupted app/call operation.

A spare pack can be prepared for the next shift, and aging batteries can be replaced without retiring the handset. The 18 W wall adapter figure does not mean every cradle charges at 18 W; the single cradle is specified with a 5 V / 2 A supply.

Dated lab record: 10 h 20 min runtime; charging 3 h 13 min at 5 V / 2 A and 2 h 41 min at 9 V / 2 A. Listed conditions include a three-second scan interval, 320-nit screen, Wi-Fi/Bluetooth enabled and 25°C. Scanner-engine, cellular and audio cells are blank. This is not a guaranteed shift length or evidence of identical workloads across models. The old shared battlecard's 4600 mAh is retained as a discrepancy; it is not an orderable current capacity.

[10] Shared use, fingerprint and charging accessories

The dedicated specification places the fingerprint sensor in the power button. The user guide includes generic face/fingerprint setup screens and an inconsistent back-side sensor description. Demonstrate fingerprint on the actual unit; do not infer a validated face-authentication function or its absence from generic UI. Device unlocking is separate from business-app login, named-user audit and shared-device session isolation.

The accessories guide lists a single cradle for one device plus one spare pack, a five-device charging cradle, rugged case with hand strap, shoulder strap, screen protector and spare battery. These accessories are not implied to be included. DT630 has USB-C 2.0 OTG and a two-pin charging interface; no display-output workstation dock is established by the reviewed guide.

Demonstrate: lock/unlock the device, apply app access rules, show the charging location and walk through a powered-off battery change. Validate user sign-out and data separation in the chosen app/MDM.

NFC conflict: memory mem_e4f0b240492247f7 (September 4 correction) says rear-only from March 18; mem_4f652e914de8fec5 (September 10 field note) says front-only. The spec lists NFC/HCE/Soft POS but no antenna face. The current user guide says to place the tag near the back-side NFC antenna (printed page 37 / PDF page 41), supporting the rear-only correction; the conflicting later field note remains unresolved. Confirm the shipping hardware before a front-tap/payment commitment. No DT610 front-display NFC demonstration is reused.

[12] AI shelf auditing: image capture plus application

DT630 provides a 50 MP rear camera for high-resolution shelf photography. A full-shelf image gives each product and price label only a small part of the frame; higher capture resolution can preserve detail useful to a compatible vision application. Lighting, angle, distance, lens quality and model training still determine recognition results.

The proposed retail workflow uses separate AI software to recognize products, detect empty facings, compare placement with a planogram and optionally read price tags by OCR. These are solution-level possibilities, not built-in DT630 functions or guaranteed recognition of every product. Validate the software, image capture protocol, assortment data and store planogram before customer deployment.

[11] Exact competitor variants and fair comparison

The supplied analysis and DT630 battlecard (January 15, 2026) establish the competitor set. Current vendor pages reviewed October 13, 2026 qualify dated entries. Differences below concern architecture and specified configuration, not guaranteed speed, cost or TCO superiority.

  • Samsung Galaxy XCover7 UK 128 GB and Samsung Galaxy XCover7 Pro launch specifications: referenced 6 GB / 128 GB configurations, 6.6-inch display and 10.2 mm body. Pro supports Wi-Fi 6E / Bluetooth 5.4. The local battlecard classifies both as camera-scanning phones; Samsung documents programmable barcode app access. Evaluate a complete phone/app/accessory solution. No Android-lifecycle superiority is claimed.
  • Zebra EM45 regular/non-RFID specification: camera-based barcode capture, service-replaceable 4750 mAh battery, 243 g, 6.7-inch display and 10.7 mm body. It also offers enterprise software and optional workstation capabilities. The older battlecard's 5000 mAh / 250 g and combined RFID cell are not copied into this comparison. EM45 RFID is a separate variant with built-in UHF, so UHF is not a unique DT630 advantage against that variant.
  • Bluebird X40 official page: 12 mm, 199 g, 6.11-inch AMOLED, camera-based capture with green-LED aiming/ToF assistance, built-in UHF and warm-swap. The current page mentions 1.5 m drop, so the old battlecard's 1.2 m is not used to claim DT630 protection superiority. X40 also has an NPU; NPU presence is not a unique DT630 advantage. Memory configurations vary across the page/battlecard.

Selection: prioritize DT630 when phone-like carrying, dedicated standard-range scans, image evidence and optional nearby RFID belong in the same workflow. A long-range warehouse scanner, continuous battery swap, workstation display dock or verified front-NFC payment requirement needs a different confirmed configuration/product evaluation.
