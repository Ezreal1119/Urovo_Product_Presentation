K388 Pro · Sources & presentation notes

Evidence checked 13 October 2026. This two-page presentation covers the Android 2-inch and 4-inch formats with one common feature narrative. The separate Bluetooth-only K388 Pro BT is outside scope. RFID is presented as an optional family capability, with configuration evidence qualified below. No competitor comparison is included.

[1] One mobile labeling workflow

The September series spec positions K388 Pro as an all-in-one solution combining scanning, printing, peeling and label application. It names excessive walking, waiting, labeling errors, label mix-ups, rework and delayed synchronization as workflow problems. The benefit is completing the task beside the goods rather than carrying batches of detached labels back from a fixed printer.

The device prepares/presents labels and supports application at the item. The slide does not depict unattended robotic application or imply that a trigger alone identifies the correct physical item. Data lookup, template selection, transaction recording and verification require the customer application.

The older brochure’s “1.5-second full labeling” is a marketing claim without a loaded test protocol; the newer series sheet does not establish a universal cycle time for both widths. It is deliberately not promised on the slide. Similarly, no measured labor reduction or ROI is invented.

Series marketing describes an anti-slip gun grip and mid-balance ergonomics. Validate carried weight and work technique over a representative shift rather than promising fatigue reduction for every operator.

Demonstrate: identify an item, retrieve its approved record, preview the label, print/peel, apply to that item and verify the output. Show the complete task rather than printer speed alone.

The Label Printer app manual documents Scan & Print, label/model settings, file import/export and RFID settings on equipped units. Barcode-only, text-only and barcode+text layouts are supported. Android 20 is the published upgrade target, not the current installed OS, a delivered update schedule or a fixed number of maintenance years from purchase. Confirm the project’s software and support terms.

A damaged or missing barcode may require a different source record or lookup; the scanner does not reconstruct every unreadable code. Dedicated specs list front 5 MP and rear 13 MP with flash/PDAF. [4] Direct thermal printing and command-language compatibility

Both dedicated specs list direct thermal, 203 dpi, CPCL/ZPL/TSPL and gap/black-mark sensing. The benefit of the language support is an opportunity to reuse validated label layouts and system interfaces. It does not establish drop-in compatibility with every template, font, driver or application.

The 2-inch maximum print width is 48 mm, with up to 120 mm/s stated print speed. The 4-inch maximum is 104 mm, with up to 100 mm/s. These differ from media widths and do not indicate the same end-to-end labeling throughput. The main slide keeps the different print-width figures beside the model images and tells the common printing story once.

The series marketing page calls narrow printing 2 inches/50.8 mm, but its specification table and dedicated spec use 48 mm as the maximum printed width. The deck uses 48 mm. The accessory log records the 4-inch speed update to 100 mm/s on August 17; older 90 mm/s values are not copied.

Direct thermal labels need appropriate paper, adhesive and storage conditions. Validate print density, barcode readability and durability for the intended lifetime. No permanent outdoor or high-heat label-life guarantee is made.

Demonstrate: print the customer’s real layout at its intended width, confirm the barcode with the downstream scanner and test one existing command-language job before asserting migration compatibility.

[5] Optional tag encoding and verification

The common sales narrative follows the user’s instruction: RFID is marked Optional, rather than creating separate feature lists. The 2-inch dedicated spec explicitly lists optional UHF tag encoding. The September series sheet spans the optional encoding row across the 2-inch/4-inch Android columns; the dedicated 4-inch sheet has no UHF row. This is an active configuration-evidence difference. Confirm the precise RFID-capable order code before quoting it; ordinary 4-inch imagery and carton labeling do not assert a fitted RFID module.

User memory mem_379e7334f5cb8f31 states the product’s UHF role is a Tag Writer, not a handheld inventory reader. It supports EPC writing with passwords, Reserved-area Access password writing, selected-area locking, EPC readback/retry and TID readback for SKU–EPC–TID association. Readback inside encoding is distinct from aisle-wide inventory scanning.

The memory describes encoding while the tag is stationary, then readback verification, optional locking and subsequent visual printing. The slide therefore says one workflow rather than claiming physical writing and printing occur simultaneously on moving media.

The app manual distinguishes RFID-equipped and normal units: without a module, RFID settings are hidden and writing is unavailable. It supports EPC-96, EPC-128 and USER formats, hexadecimal auto-increment and SGTIN-96 from scanned EAN-13. Select a format that the tag supports; correct retail serialization and backend association still require the application/data design.

Optional RFID does not guarantee every tag or chip, a reading/writing distance, anti-counterfeiting, universal write protection or permanent locking. Validate compatible consumables, region and authorized password/lock settings. No encoding capability is used to suggest general RFID stocktaking.

Demonstrate: write the intended EPC, read it back, associate TID if required, simulate an encoding failure and prove that the workflow identifies/retries/rejects it. Then verify visual content and apply the correct label.

[6] Hot-swap and shared battery preparation

Both Android dedicated specs explicitly support hot-swap with a 10.8 V 3000 mAh pack. Both list an optional four-slot battery charger. These support preparing charged spares and keeping the terminal powered while exchanging a battery.

Device power continuity does not by itself validate exchanging a battery during an active paper-feed or RFID-write operation. Follow the supported swap procedure and verify application state in the actual workflow. No swap time or unlimited reserve-power window is specified in the loaded sources.

The series sheet’s 18–20-hour runtime is marketing wording without a loaded test method; no runtime-hours guarantee is used. Source pack energy is printed as 32.24 Wh in specs and 32.4 Wh in some marketing, so the visible slide avoids selecting an energy figure. Capacity at 10.8 V must not be compared directly with a phone’s mAh figure.

The accessory workbook lists a shared spare-pack code and four-battery chargers with regional adapters. Confirm the correct pack/charger and official operating procedure.

[7] Roll handling, sensing and media fit

The series sheet describes a quick-release paper magazine and forward/reverse label rolls. Both dedicated specs list gap and black-mark sensors, roll outer diameter up to 60 mm and a 25.4 mm core. Load the intended media, select the correct layout and calibrate sensing to position each label consistently.

Dedicated media width ranges differ: 30–58 mm for 2-inch and 50–118 mm for 4-inch. These are roll/media widths, not maximum printed image widths. The common feature narrative does not imply the two formats share every roll, magazine or media part number.

Accessory-guide sample capacities such as 473 or 360 labels depend on the particular dimensions, paper/liner thickness and roll geometry; they are not a universal per-roll count. RFID consumables are ordered by roll, not by individual tag. Do not carry those sample counts into every media calculation.

Demonstrate: reload the customer’s actual roll, calibrate gap/black-mark detection, print several consecutive labels and check peel/application behavior on the intended product surface.
